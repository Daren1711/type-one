A manifesto for the builders of a Type One civilisation

I. The inheritance

In a single hour, our planet catches more energy from its star than our whole civilisation uses in a year.

Today, more than thirteen thousand children under five will die. Most of them will die of things we already know how to prevent.

Both of those sentences are true, on the same planet, on the same day. Read them again. If the second one does not make you angry, read it until it does.

We were raised on a lie. The lie says: expect less. Less room, less money, less future than your parents had. Lower your hopes, mind your own life, and be grateful for whatever is left.

We were handed the most powerful tools any generation has ever held, and with them a single instruction: sit down.

We refuse.

We are the first generation with the tools to end scarcity. We are also the first to be told the future is cancelled. Only one of those will be remembered as the truth about us. This manifesto is our choice of which.

It is written for my generation and for those who come after it. It is written for anyone who suspects that the life they have been offered is too small.

II. What we see

We see a species that knows how to feed, heal and power every one of its members, and chooses not to finish the job.

Count it. Eight hundred million people live in extreme poverty. Six hundred and forty-five million went hungry last year. More than half of humanity, 4.6 billion people, cannot get the basic healthcare they need.

Now count this. In that same year the governments of the world spent 2.9 trillion dollars on their armies. It was the highest figure ever recorded.

Do not call that a shortage of money. Do not call it a shortage of knowledge. It is a choice. It is made again every year, by people who will never feel what it costs.

We see wars fought over borders, verses and bloodlines by people who share one planet and one fate.

We see Palestinians denied a homeland while the world answers with hashtags. We see corruption steal the schools and hospitals of the countries that need them most, my own among them. A stolen budget is not a number. It is a clinic with no medicine, and a child taught in a room with no roof.

We see human beings ranked by gender, by whom they love, by skin and by creed. By everything except what they have built for others.

We see faiths that were meant to make people better used as reasons to make enemies.

And we see ourselves. This is the part nobody wants to read.

We see outrage that costs nothing. A post. A slogan. A march that ends in time for dinner. We have confused being seen to care with caring. No generation has ever known so much about the world's suffering, or done so little with its own hands.

Meanwhile the rooms where decisions are made stay empty of the people who care the most. The corrupt are not afraid of your post. They are afraid of your candidacy, your audit and your company.

We see talent spent on comfort. Some of the best minds of our time are paid to make strangers look at advertisements. We see a ceiling mistaken for a floor.

We do not accept this as the natural condition of humanity. It is a habit. Habits can be broken. And we would rather be disliked for breaking it than thanked for keeping it.

III. Why now

The window is open, and it will not stay open.

More than half the world's countries now have too few births to hold their numbers. Japan's births have fallen for ten years running. The United Nations expects the human population to peak in the 2080s and then shrink. We may be among the largest young generations this planet will ever hold.

A civilisation that is ageing and shrinking does not launch great projects. It manages decline. The great works must be started while there are still enough of us young enough to start them.

And for the first time, the tools exist. Rockets land and fly again. Private companies are sending the first prospectors to asteroids. Engineers are beaming power through the air to prove it can be sent down from orbit. Machines are learning to do work that no human could survive and no payroll could afford.

The first attempts are failing. That is what first attempts do. The second and third are already on the launch pad.

A closing window and an open door. That is the meaning of now. Nobody who reads this gets to say they were not told.

IV. What we believe

  1. Scarcity is an engineering problem. It is not a law of nature. There is no shortage of matter or energy in the solar system. There is only a shortage of reach.
  2. Energy is the root of every other abundance. Clean water, food, medicine and shelter are all energy in another form. Make energy abundant and poverty, hunger and preventable disease become problems of logistics.
  3. Earth is the cradle, not the limit. The metals we fight over lie in the asteroids in quantities no mine on Earth can match. The Sun pours out more power than we can yet imagine using. Heavy industry belongs in space. The garden stays here.
  4. AI is the lever. Today it draws power and resources. That is a loan, and we intend to see it repaid many times over: by machines that mine where no human can live, and build what no workforce could. Our task is to make sure it is repaid to everyone.
  5. A long life is a long contribution. We will extend healthy human life, so that a mind at one hundred can still learn, build and teach. The work has begun. In 2025 OpenAI and the longevity company Retro Biosciences built an AI model to redesign the proteins that turn old cells young again. In early lab tests its designs worked fifty times better than nature's. It is early, and it is only a laboratory result. But it shows the road: machines that read biology faster than any human can. Every healthy decade we add is another chance for a person to fulfil their potential and build something larger than themselves.
  6. We are one species. No one's worth is settled by birth, body, love or creed. It is only added to by what they do for others.
  7. Action is the only proof of belief. What you say you care about is a rumour until you have paid for it in years of work.
  8. Be grateful, and never satisfied. We owe everything to those who built before us. The only honest thanks is to build further.

Why space, when Earth has problems?

This is the best objection to everything above, and it deserves a straight answer. Money and talent are limited. A dollar sent to orbit is a dollar not spent on a clinic today. Whoever says that is right about this year. We think they are wrong about this century.

Most of Earth's problems are, at root, problems of scarcity: too little energy, too little material, too little room. You cannot end scarcity from inside the box that causes it. And the choice was never one or the other. Our first deadlines are on the ground: electricity in every home, and extreme poverty below one person in a hundred.

Nor is space where the money goes. The United States spends about 0.4 percent of its federal budget on NASA. We are not choosing the stars over the hungry. We are going to the stars for them.

V. What we will build

Our goal is a Type I civilisation: a humanity that commands energy on the scale of its whole planet, and uses it for the good of every person on it. Today we use about a five-hundredth of that.

To see what that means, picture one village. Tonight it has a few hours of electricity, if the grid holds. Now suppose power cost that village almost nothing. The well pumps itself. The clinic's refrigerator keeps its vaccines cold. The school has light after dark, and a girl who used to carry water spends that hour reading.

Nothing in that picture needs a new invention. It needs energy cheap enough to waste. Multiply that village by every village on Earth, and you have the first half of Type One.

How we get there.

Energy first. A solar panel in orbit sees the Sun almost without night, cloud or season. The power can be sent down as a beam and caught by a receiver on the ground. In 2026 engineers beamed power from a moving aircraft. The next rungs are a megawatt from orbit, then a gigawatt. Each rung has already been planned by someone. We intend to climb them faster.

Then materials. An asteroid is too far away to steer a machine by radio, so the miners must think for themselves. That is why AI matters. First we prospect. Then we bring back a sample. Then we refine in space, and use the first water and metal out there, as fuel and as structure, before a single tonne is sent home.

Then people. The wealth flows through the fund into schools, clinics and power, until the places that were left behind are building alongside us.

These are our dates. Hold us to them.

By 2035

  • Power beamed from orbit to the ground at the scale of a power plant's first megawatt.
  • The first asteroid metal brought back by a private company.

By 2050

  • The first gigawatt power station in orbit, selling electricity to Earth.
  • Asteroid mining that pays for itself.
  • Electricity in every home on Earth, and extreme poverty below one person in a hundred. Today it is nearly one in ten.

By 2075

  • Basic healthcare for every human being.
  • A healthy, working life to one hundred as ordinary medicine.

By 2100

  • A settlement beyond Earth that could survive without us.
  • Ten times today's energy, clean, for a humanity that no longer rations it.

Beyond

  • A Type I civilisation. On today's trend it arrives in the 2300s. Our purpose is to beat the trend.
  • The full light of the Sun put to work.
  • The stars, at whatever speed physics allows. If the laws permit a faster road, we will find it. If they do not, we will build slower ships. The stars are patient.

Where we could be wrong.

To be clear, none of this is certain. We would rather say so now than be caught pretending later.

  • The dates are our best estimates. They are not prophecies. First attempts at hard things usually run late, and some of ours will.
  • Power from orbit may lose the race. Solar panels and batteries on the ground get cheaper every year, and it may turn out that Earth can be powered well without leaving it. If so, good. The goal is abundant energy. It is not a favourite machine.
  • Asteroid mining has never paid for itself. Nobody yet knows whether it will by 2050.
  • The stars may be beyond any ship we can build. Physics does not owe us a faster road.

What will not change is the direction. If a date slips, we will move the date and publish why. If a method fails, we will change the method. The one thing we will not do is lower the goal.

And it will be shared.

Abundance that reaches only its owners is not abundance. It is the old inequality with a longer reach.

Let those who take the risk own what they mine, up to a limit: no single owner may control more than a capped share of any resource beyond Earth. And a fixed share of everything taken from space belongs to every human being. It will be held in a permanent fund, run by an independent world body and open to audit by anyone on Earth.

That fund will not hand out charity. It will build capability: schools, teachers, clinics and power. Feed a child so that she can learn. Teach her so that she never needs feeding again.

And we will not wait for the law. Every company we found will be bound by its charter to give a fixed share of what it earns to the people it was built to serve.

And it will be one.

Borders were drawn to guard what was scarce: land, water, oil, grain. When nothing is scarce, there is nothing left for a border to guard.

We do not ask anyone to give up who they are. Languages, faiths, cuisines, songs and traditions are the human inheritance, and we will carry every one of them to the stars. Culture is what we keep. The border is what we outgrow.

So our last goal on this planet is a united Earth: one people, many cultures, no walls. It will not be imposed by any empire. It will be chosen, by a humanity that no longer has a reason to be afraid of itself.

VI. The code

Anger got you this far down the page. It will not get you one step further. Anger without a plan is a tantrum, and the people who run the world have learned to wait out tantrums.

From here on, only work counts. A goal this large is reached by stepping stones. Each of us lives by the same code.

  1. Choose one problem worth a lifetime. Not five. One.
  2. Work backwards from it to where you stand today. There is always a next step within reach. Take it.
  3. Acquire what the goal requires. If it needs skill, study. If it needs capital, learn how capital is made. If it needs power, earn office. Choose your degree and your work for where they lead, not for how they sound.
  4. Go where the decisions are made. If you believe in Palestine's right to exist, do not stop at the vigil. If you hate corruption, do not stop at the sign. Study the law. Enter the ministry. Win the seat. Write the policy. March if it moves you, then go to work.
  5. Start from where you are. No one is coming. Your starting point is not your fault. Your next step is your responsibility. Those of us who start with more owe more.
  6. Keep the account. Each year, ask what exists because of you that did not exist before.
  7. Live fully. Love, rest and joy are not betrayals of the mission. They are what the mission is for. But do not mistake comfortable for finished.

Find your part.

Belief without a task is a mood. So here is the task, by trade.

  • If you are an engineer or a scientist: the open problems are known. Robots that prospect and mine with no human at the controls. Power beamed safely from orbit. Cheaper launch. The biology of ageing. Pick one. This month, join a team that works on it: a lab, a student space society, an open-source project. If none exists near you, start one with two friends.
  • If you work in finance or business: these ventures die for lack of patient money, not for lack of ideas. Learn to fund things that take twenty years to pay. This month, study how one hard-technology company was financed, from its first cheque to its first revenue.
  • If you are a lawyer or a policymaker: no one has written the rules for mining another world or for sharing what comes back. The treaty that governs space dates from 1967. Write the next one. This month, read it. It is seventeen articles long. And at home, run for something: a student council, a city council, a parliament.
  • If you are a writer, an artist or a filmmaker: people cannot build a future they cannot picture. This month, make one thing that shows it to them.
  • If you are a teacher: this term, teach one lesson on what humanity could do, not only what it has done. One of your students will build it.
  • If you are none of these: you are still a voter, a customer, a parent, a friend. Vote for builders. Buy from builders. Raise builders. And live by the code.

Three deadlines for every reader.

  1. Within a week, write down your one problem and sign your name under it.
  2. Within a month, find four other people who care about it, and meet.
  3. Within a year, finish one real thing, however small, and publish what you learned.

Miss a deadline and begin again the next morning. That is all a builder is: someone who begins again.

VII. What we will never do

A goal this large has tempted people before us into terrible things. So we bind ourselves now, in public.

  1. We will never kill or harm to advance this cause. No violence, no terror, no war. A future built on bodies is not the one we want.
  2. We will never take what belongs to others. No conquest, no theft, no empire, on Earth or beyond it.
  3. We will never lie about our results. We report what happened, failures first.
  4. We will never buy or sell influence. Corruption is the disease we came to cure.
  5. We will never wreck one world to reach another. We will not poison the Earth to leave it. We will remove more mass from orbit than we add. Any world found to hold life, we leave alone.
  6. We will never build a machine that answers to no one. No autonomous system of ours will act, on Earth or in space, without a human able to stop it.
  7. We will never force anyone to join us. A united Earth is chosen or it is nothing.

Do not mistake these limits for softness.

The corrupt understand one language, and it is power. So we will learn to speak it fluently, without becoming them.

  • We will seek power in the open: office, capital and influence. Power is not evil. Leaving it to the worst people is.
  • We will compete to win. We will out-work them, out-build them and out-organise them. We will take their voters, their customers and their best people.
  • We will use every lawful weapon: the audit, the lawsuit, the published record, the ballot and the boycott.
  • We will name the corrupt in public, with evidence.
  • We will ally with people we dislike when the goal requires it.
  • We will outlast them. They plan for the next election. We plan for the next century.

Hard, never dirty. The day we bribe, lie or harm "for the greater good", we have only built one more corrupt machine, and the people we set out to defeat will have new names: ours.

If the goal can only be reached by crossing these lines, we would rather arrive late.

VIII. Who is writing this

I grew up in Indonesia, a country of enormous wealth and enormous poverty living side by side. I watched people I love struggle in a land that has everything.

My father's family once held land across Klungkung and Nusa Penida in Bali. In 1963 Mount Agung erupted, and they lost it. My grandmother went to work in the market. She saved, coin by coin, until she could send her son to university in Jakarta. He lived poor, worked his way up, and built a company from nothing.

That is where I learned what one person's effort can do. It is also where I learned how much is left undone. My father came to know some of the most powerful people in the country, and he was too proud to ask any of them for anything. I respect that pride. I also know what it cost: the good he could have done with a single request.

I have seen great ability go unused, in my own family and in my own generation. I have watched young Indonesians fill the streets against corruption, then go home while the parliament stayed exactly as it was. Not one of those seats is closed to them.

Wasted potential is the thing I cannot stand. This manifesto is my answer to it.

My name is Made Gde Darendra Putrawan. Most people call me Daren. I was born in Jakarta in 2007. As I write this I am nineteen, in a foundation year at the University of Leeds, on my way to a degree in finance and economics. I have built nothing yet, and I will not pretend otherwise.

Yes, a nineteen-year-old wrote this. Ask yourself why it was left to one. The people with the money, the offices and the experience have had decades to write it. They wrote quarterly reports instead.

I chose to study capital because everything above is a financing problem before it is an engineering problem. My plan is three stones long, and each stone has a date.

By 2030, I graduate, knowing how capital moves. By 2035, I will have founded a company in the infrastructure of that decade: AI computing, or whatever the frontier is by then. By 2045, I will use what it earns to found or fund a company that mines the asteroids or harvests the Sun.

I should be plain about my own interest. If this works, I stand to become wealthy, and I will not pretend that means nothing to me. That is exactly why the charter comes first.

Whatever I build will carry that charter from its first day. A fixed share of it will belong to a foundation for the people this manifesto is written for. I will name the share before I take a single investor.

Each step pays for the next. I am publishing this so that you can hold me to it.

IX. The call

To my generation: you have been called distracted, anxious and lost. You have been called the generation that scrolls.

Some of it is true. That is why it stings.

So here is the question this whole manifesto was written to ask. In fifty years a child will ask what you did with the most powerful tools any generation ever held. What will you tell her? That you were busy? That you posted about it?

To those who told us to expect less: we heard you. We decline.

To the corrupt: we are not coming with signs. We are coming for your seats, by the ballot and by name.

To everyone who is tired of being angry while nothing changes: your anger was never the problem. It is fuel. It has only ever been pointed at a screen.

Put down the argument. Pick up the work. Choose your problem, lay your first stone, and find the others who are laying theirs.

You have nothing to lose but your excuses.

We have a star to harness, worlds to reach and one short life each to do it in. Let it be said of us that we were handed a cancelled future, and built one anyway.

We call the work Type One. We call ourselves builders. Sign your name beside ours.

The future is not promised. It is built.

Come and build it.

Daren Putrawan, Leeds, 2026
